# Facebook Marketplace scam and red-flag patterns

Listings lie. Listing cards (the thumbnail summary FB shows on the
results page) lie more than listing bodies, because FB pulls structured
metadata from form fields the seller filled out and the seller can put
whatever they want.

Roughly half of the most appealing-looking Marketplace listings in a
typical search have at least one significant red flag visible only in
the body text or the seller's profile. Filtering on the card alone is
not enough.

## Outright scams (walk immediately)

These mean the listing is not a real private sale and is either bait for
a wire-transfer scam, a stolen-car flip, or a salvage rebuild masquerading
as a clean car.

- **Price 30%+ below comparable listings.** A 2014 Camry LE with 90K
  miles selling for $3,800 in a market where comparable cars are $8K is
  bait. Either the title is salvage, the car is stolen, or the seller
  is going to ask for a deposit and disappear.
- **"Selling for a friend / mother / deceased relative / military
  deployment."** The escape-hatch story. Used so the seller doesn't have
  to know any service history and can disappear without trace. Walk
  unless extraordinary evidence (estate paperwork visible).
- **"Will ship the car for you, send payment to my eBay/PayPal/Zelle."**
  100% scam. FB Marketplace is local pickup.
- **"I'm out of state but the car is in [your city] at a storage
  facility, you can pick it up after payment."** 100% scam.
- **VIN refused.** Real owners give the VIN over message. Curbstoners
  and scammers refuse.
- **Photos look like stock images or borrowed from another listing.**
  Reverse-image search on Google. If the photos appear on AutoTrader,
  CarGurus, or another Marketplace listing in a different city, walk.
- **Listing posted within the last 24 hours and price already reduced
  twice.** The seller is panicking; the car has a problem.

## Body-text red flags (walk or treat as project car)

- **"Rebuilt title" / "Salvage title" / "Branded title"**: project car.
  Insurance is harder. Resale is harder. Mechanical state is unknown.
- **"Engine swap" / "swapped engine" / "JDM swap" / "head replaced"**:
  the original engine failed and was replaced with an unknown one.
  Documentation is rare. Walk unless user is shopping projects.
- **"Buy here pay here" / "BHPH" / "in-house financing"**: this is a
  dealer (illegal as a "private seller"). The car has been through a
  BHPH lot, which means it was repo'd at least once. Walk.
- **"As is" stated multiple times with emphasis**: the seller knows
  something is wrong. Ask what.
- **"Cash only" with urgency**: fine on its own, but combined with other
  flags is bad.
- **"No tire-kickers" / "serious inquiries only"**: this is usually fine
  and even a green flag, but if combined with marketing copy elsewhere
  in the listing it is performance.
- **"Runs and drives great" with no other detail**: curbstoner filler.
  A real owner says "runs great, just had the timing belt done at
  100K, oil changes every 5K at the dealer." Specifics are signal.
- **"Some issues" / "needs minor work" / "small problem"** without
  naming the issue: the issue is not minor. Ask.
- **"Mechanic's special"**: car is broken, seller is hoping you fix it.
  Walk unless user is a mechanic.
- **"Recently inspected" with no date**: anyone can say this. Ask for
  the inspection paperwork.

## Body-text yellow flags (verify, don't auto-walk)

- **"AC blows cold"**: fine in summer; in winter this phrase is a tell
  that the seller is anticipating the buyer's first concern. Verify.
- **"Aftermarket exhaust" / "aftermarket intake"**: car may have been
  driven hard. Inspect carefully.
- **"Original paint" claimed but car has uneven panel gaps**: ask about
  accident history. Could be repaint cover-up.
- **"Selling because I'm moving"**: half real, half curbstoner filler.
  Verify with seller-profile check.
- **"Just had X service done"**: ask for the receipt. If they have it,
  they will share it. If they don't, the service didn't happen.

## Body-text GREEN flags (proceed with confidence)

These signal a real owner who has lived with the car:

- **Specific dated maintenance.** "Replaced front struts in March 2024
  at City Honda, $850, receipt available." Specifics + receipts = real.
- **Honest cosmetic disclosure.** "Front bumper has a scuff from a
  parking lot encounter, didn't bother fixing it." Real owners disclose
  the small stuff.
- **Honest mechanical disclosure.** "AC isn't blowing cold, probably
  needs a recharge or a compressor; priced accordingly." Real owners
  who couldn't be bothered to fix something tell you, because they
  don't want a return.
- **Personal context for the sale.** "Bought this in 2020 for my
  commute, switched jobs and now work from home, doesn't make sense to
  keep two cars."
- **Salty private-seller tone.** "NO DEALERS!! NO TRADES!! NO LOWBALLS!!
  PRICE IS FIRM!!" Curbstoners are nice. Real annoyed-by-tire-kickers
  owners are not.
- **Detailed spec.** "EX-L trim, leather, sunroof, heated seats, premium
  audio, no nav. Bridgestone tires installed Feb 2024."

## Price-too-good-to-be-true math

A useful sanity check: pull KBB private-party values for the year/make/
model/trim/mileage in the user's region. If the listing is **below the
"Fair" private-party value by more than 15%**, ask what is wrong with
the car.

Below 30% of KBB Fair is bait. The car either does not exist or has a
hidden problem big enough to be the entire purchase price.

## Listing-card vs body-text divergence

The listing card pulls from the form fields the seller filled out.
Sellers routinely:

- Pick "Excellent" condition when body text describes obvious issues.
- Pick a higher trim than the car actually is.
- Pick "Clean title" when the car has a branded title (this is fraud;
  it's also common).
- Pick a higher mpg estimate than the car achieves.

Always read the body text. Always look at the photos. Always verify the
trim independently (see `trim-id-guide.md`).

## What to do when in doubt

If a listing has a single yellow flag and a strong seller profile, it's
probably fine. Verify the flag with the seller before driving anywhere.

If a listing has two or more flags, or a single red flag, walk. There
are always more listings.

The user's time is the scarce resource. A wasted Saturday on a
curbstoner's car is more expensive than three good cars they didn't see.
