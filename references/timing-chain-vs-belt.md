# Timing chain vs timing belt

The timing belt or chain keeps the crankshaft and camshaft(s) in sync.
If it breaks on an "interference" engine (most modern engines), the
pistons hit the valves and the engine is destroyed.

- **Timing chain**: lubricated by engine oil, runs inside the engine,
  generally lasts the life of the engine if oil is changed regularly.
  No scheduled replacement.
- **Timing belt**: rubber, runs outside the engine block (under a cover),
  has a scheduled replacement interval of 60K to 105K miles. Cost to
  replace varies by engine: $500 to $900 for a 4-cyl belt-only job;
  $900 to $1,600 for a V6 "do-it-all" service (belt + water pump +
  tensioner + idler + seals), which is the typical bundle.

For a used-car buyer, the math is:

- Chain engine: zero scheduled timing service. Cost-of-ownership win.
- Belt engine: factor the next belt service into the purchase price.
  If the belt is overdue on a V6, that's a $900+ negotiation lever
  (and a walk if the seller refuses to discount and you find any
  belt-failure damage on the PPI).

**Belt is not auto-disqualifying.** It is a known cost. Price it in.

## Special cases

- **Honda J37 3.7L V6** (later MDX, RL): chain. Reliable.
- **Honda J35Z / J35Y** (2008+ Accord V6, 2007+ MDX, 2009+ Pilot,
  Ridgeline 2nd gen): chain. The J35A belt motors are pre-2008.
- **Subaru EJ25 DOHC turbo** (WRX, Forester XT, Legacy GT, Outback XT):
  belt + boost + premium fuel + carbon issues. Avoid unless user wants
  a project.
- **Audi 2.0T TFSI / TSI**: chain, but the chain tensioner is known to
  fail, taking out the engine. Skip pre-2013.
- **VW / Audi diesel TDI**: belt, expensive service. Skip.
- **BMW N52 / N54 / N55 I6**: chain. N54 has injector and HPFP issues.
- **Mini Cooper 1.6L Prince engine** (shared with Peugeot): chain
  tensioner failure. Skip.

## How to check on a specific listing

1. Decode the VIN to confirm engine code (8th digit on most modern cars).
2. Cross-reference against the tables above.
3. If belt: ask the seller "has the timing belt been done? At what
   mileage? Do you have the receipt?"
4. If yes with receipt: green flag. Belt was done recently.
5. If no or "I think so but no receipt": treat as not done. Budget
   $700 to $1,200 into the purchase price.
6. If chain: skip the belt question; ask about other model-specific
   issues instead.

## Why this matters

A 2005 Lexus ES 330 (belt, $400 typical asking premium over an ES 350)
with no belt service in 100K is actually $500 more expensive than an
ES 350 at the same ask, once you factor in the belt service. Most
buyers don't run this math; you can.
