# Insurance coverage by vehicle ACV

For a used car under $15K, the standard auto-insurance script (full
coverage with low deductibles) is wrong. You can save hundreds of
dollars per year on premiums and put that money toward maintenance.

This guide covers what to keep and what to drop, organized by the
actual cash value (ACV) of the car.

## The coverage types

Every US auto policy is a stack of these. Some are mandatory; most are
optional. Know what each one does before deciding.

| Coverage | What it pays | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| Bodily injury liability | Damage to other people if you cause an accident | Mandatory (most states) |
| Property damage liability | Damage to others' property if you cause an accident | Mandatory (most states) |
| Uninsured / underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) | Your medical bills if someone with no/low insurance hits you | Mandatory in some states |
| Medical payments (MedPay) / PIP | Your medical bills regardless of fault | Mandatory in no-fault states |
| Collision | Damage to your car in a crash (any cause, including your fault) | Optional |
| Comprehensive (Comp) | Damage to your car from theft, vandalism, weather, animal, fire | Optional |
| Rental reimbursement | Rental car while yours is in the shop | Optional |
| Roadside | Tow, jump-start, lockout | Optional |
| Gap | The difference between ACV and loan payoff (loaned cars only) | Optional |

## Liability: always max out

Liability is the cheapest coverage by far. It exists to prevent you
from being sued personally for damages exceeding the policy limit. Most
states set the minimum at $25K bodily injury per person / $50K per
accident / $25K property. **That is not enough.**

A serious crash with injuries can run $100K+ in medical bills. If
liability runs out, the other party can sue you for your assets,
including future wages.

**Recommended minimum:** $100K / $300K / $100K. **Better:** $250K /
$500K / $250K. The premium difference between state minimum and $250K
limits is usually $100 to $200 per year. Cheapest insurance you can
buy.

If you own a house or have meaningful savings, consider an **umbrella
policy** ($1M for ~$200 per year) on top. Out of scope for this guide
but worth mentioning.

## Uninsured / underinsured motorist: keep at high limits

UM/UIM pays for **your** injuries when the at-fault driver doesn't have
enough coverage (or any coverage). In some states 1 in 8 drivers is
uninsured.

Match your UM/UIM limits to your liability limits. Premium impact is
small. This is the coverage that protects you and your family from
other people's bad insurance.

## Medical payments / PIP: state-dependent

In **no-fault states** (NY, FL, MI, NJ, PA, MA, KY, KS, ND, MN, UT,
HI), PIP is mandatory. Get the minimum required and lean on health
insurance for the rest, unless you have a high-deductible health plan.

In **at-fault states** (most), MedPay is optional. $5K of MedPay is
usually $20 to $50/year and covers ambulance + ER copays out of pocket
regardless of fault. Useful if your health insurance has a high
deductible.

## Comprehensive (comp): keep

Comp covers theft, weather, vandalism, animal strikes, fire, falling
trees. Premiums are low (typically $100 to $200 per year on an older
car) and the deductible is your choice.

**Keep comp until the car's ACV is below ~$2,000.** Below that, the
premium is a meaningful fraction of the payout cap.

Set the deductible to $500 or $1,000 to keep the premium down. Going
from $250 to $1,000 deductible typically cuts comp premium by 30 to
40%.

## Collision: this is the key decision

Collision covers your car in any crash, including ones you caused, in
exchange for your deductible.

**The math:** collision premium is typically 40 to 60% of total premium.
On a 10-year-old car worth $5,000 with a $500 deductible, the most
collision will ever pay out is **$4,500** (ACV minus deductible). If
collision is costing $500 per year, you're paying $500 a year for an
expected payout of much less than $500 (since you don't total your car
every year).

**Drop collision when ACV is below ~$5,000.** Self-insure by setting
aside the saved premium. After two years of dropped collision, you'd
have saved $1,000+; that's most of the way to the replacement cost on
a car at that price point.

**Keep collision when:**
- The car is your only car and you cannot easily replace it
- You financed the car and the lender requires it (most lenders do)
- ACV is above $5,000 to $7,000

**Drop collision when:**
- ACV is below $5,000
- You can absorb the loss of the car without major hardship
- The premium savings will be set aside, not spent

This single decision saves $400 to $800 per year on a typical sub-$6K
private-party used car.

## Gap insurance: only if financed

Gap covers the difference between your car's ACV (what insurance pays
on a total loss) and what you still owe on the loan. Only relevant if
you financed.

For a private-party purchase under $15K, most buyers pay cash. If you
financed: get gap if you put less than 20% down. Drop it once you owe
less than the car's ACV.

## Rental reimbursement and roadside

- **Rental reimbursement:** $30 to $80 per year for $30/day for 30 days.
  Worth it if you don't have a second car. Skip if you do.
- **Roadside:** $10 to $50 per year. Cheaper through your existing AAA
  membership or credit card if you have one. Skip if redundant.

## Deductibles: go high

Higher deductible = lower premium. The breakeven is roughly:

- $250 to $500 deductible: 10 to 15% premium reduction
- $500 to $1,000 deductible: another 10 to 15%
- $1,000 to $2,000 deductible: another 10 to 15%

Set deductibles at the highest level you could absorb in cash today
without hardship. For most users that's $1,000.

## Recommended setup for a $4,000 to $7,000 used car

```
Bodily injury liability:       $250K / $500K
Property damage liability:     $100K
Uninsured/Underinsured (UM):   $250K / $500K
Medical Payments (if at-fault state): $5K
PIP (if no-fault state):       state minimum
Comprehensive:                 KEEP, $1,000 deductible
Collision:                     DROP if ACV under $5K, else $1,000 deductible
Gap:                           N/A if cash purchase
Rental reimbursement:          skip (or $30/day max if no second car)
Roadside:                      skip (use AAA / credit card)
```

**Single-car carve-out for dropping collision:** the "drop collision
under $5K ACV" rule assumes you can absorb the loss without major
hardship. If this is your only car and replacing it within a week is
not financially or logistically feasible, keep collision even at lower
ACVs (and set the deductible to $1,000 to hold the premium down). The
math changes when "total loss" means "cannot get to work tomorrow."

Expected annual premium: $700 to $1,200 depending on driving record,
location, and age. Compared to the typical "full coverage" pitch at
$1,500 to $2,500, this saves $500 to $1,300 per year.

## State-specific notes

- **Virginia** has a tort system and previously allowed uninsured drivers
  to pay a $500 fee instead of carrying insurance; this changed in 2024
  and full coverage is now required. UM/UIM is mandatory; keep at high
  limits.
- **New Hampshire** is the only state with no mandatory insurance, but
  you're still liable for damages; carry liability anyway.
- **Michigan** has unlimited medical PIP (lifetime medical for serious
  injuries). The 2019 reform lets you opt out down to $50K if you have
  good health insurance.
- **California, Florida** have high uninsured-motorist rates; max out
  UM/UIM.

## Shopping the policy

Get quotes from at least 3 carriers. Premiums vary by carrier by 50%+
for identical coverage. Major options:

- GEICO, Progressive, State Farm: largest carriers, baseline pricing
- Allstate, Liberty Mutual, Farmers: usually 10 to 30% more expensive
- USAA: military / veteran families only, typically cheapest
- Erie, Auto-Owners: regional carriers that often beat the nationals;
  available in select states only
- Costco Insurance (Connect/Ameriprise): often competitive for Costco
  members

Re-shop every 2 to 3 years. Insurers raise rates on existing customers
faster than new ones.
