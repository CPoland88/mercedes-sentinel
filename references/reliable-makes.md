# Reliable makes and models

This is a heuristic tier list for used cars in the roughly $2K to $15K
private-party band. It is biased toward "boring, long-lived, cheap to fix"
rather than "fun to drive." Adjust for the user's stated priorities.

## Tier 1: buy with confidence (still verify the specific car)

These models have generations where the drivetrain is famously durable to
250K+ miles with normal maintenance. Verify the specific year/engine combo
in `timing-chain-vs-belt.md` and in this file's known-issues section.

**Compact / midsize sedans and hatchbacks**

- Toyota Corolla (any post-2003 generation)
- Toyota Camry (4-cyl 2AZ-FE and later 2AR-FE; V6 1MZ-FE needs timing belt)
- Toyota Avalon (any generation)
- Toyota Matrix (2003 to 2014)
- Toyota Prius (Gen 2 and Gen 3; hybrid battery is the only big risk)
- Toyota Yaris / Echo
- Honda Civic (post-1996, the 8th and 9th gen R18 motors are exceptional)
- Honda Accord (4-cyl K24 in 7th and 8th gen is bulletproof; V6 J35 needs
  belt pre-2008)
- Honda Fit (any generation)
- Honda Insight (2nd gen 2010 to 2014; IMA battery is the risk)
- Mazda3 (any pre-2014 with naturally-aspirated MZR; skip SkyActiv-D)
- Mazda6 (pre-2014 4-cyl)
- Mazda2
- Mazda Protege (2.0L)
- Subaru Impreza (post-2012 FB20 SOHC; pre-2012 EJ25 has head-gasket risk)
- Subaru Legacy (4-cyl post-2010 FB25)
- Subaru Outback (4-cyl post-2010 FB25)
- Lexus ES (300/330 has 1MZ/3MZ belt; ES 350 has 2GR-FE chain)
- Lexus IS (IS250 has 4GR-FE chain, IS300 has 2JZ legend)
- Lexus GS (300/350 chain; older GS 430 chain)
- Acura TL (3rd and 4th gen)
- Acura TSX (any; K24 chain)
- Acura ILX (early gen is a Civic in a suit)
- Scion xA / xB / xD (Toyota underneath)
- Pontiac Vibe (literal Toyota Matrix with a Pontiac badge, including the
  GM dealership warranty history; often cheaper than the Matrix)

**Small / midsize SUVs**

- Honda CR-V (any pre-2017 K-series; turbo 1.5L post-2017 has known oil
  dilution issues)
- Toyota RAV4 (4-cyl any generation, including the 2AZ oil-burn caveat
  for 2007 to 2009)
- Toyota Highlander (V6 chain)
- Toyota 4Runner (V6 1GR-FE chain; pre-2009 V8 has 2UZ belt)
- Toyota Venza
- Subaru Forester (FB25 post-2011)
- Mazda CX-5 (SkyActiv-G 2.0 and 2.5)
- Mazda CX-9 (post-2016 SkyActiv-G; skip pre-2016 Ford Cyclone V6)
- Lexus RX (RX 350 with 2GR-FE chain; RX 300/330 has belt)
- Lexus NX
- Lexus GX (V8 4.7L 2UZ belt service is real money)
- Honda Pilot (4th gen onward; J35 V6 belt pre-2008)
- Acura MDX (post-2007 chain; pre-2007 belt)
- Acura RDX (any)

## Tier 2: grandpa-car archetype

Older full-size and near-luxury models with low miles, often sold by an
estate or a senior downsizing. Drivetrains are simple, parts are cheap, and
the previous owner drove them gently. Trade-off: fuel economy and styling.

- Toyota Avalon (any year, especially pre-2010)
- Lexus ES 300 / 330 / 350
- Buick LeSabre (3800 V6 is one of the most durable engines GM ever made)
- Buick Lucerne (CX trim with 3.9L V6; avoid CXS with Northstar V8)
- Buick Park Avenue
- Lincoln Town Car (4.6L Modular V8; tank-grade durability)
- Mercury Grand Marquis (same platform as Town Car)
- Ford Crown Victoria (same platform; civilian version)
- Cadillac DeVille / DTS (Northstar V8 has head-gasket risk; verify
  service history)

These are explicitly **in scope** for the user's search unless they have
specifically said they want something newer or sportier.

## Risky: avoid unless the listing is exceptional

These models have known generational defects, expensive parts, or both.
"Risky" does not mean "always bad." A specific car with full records and a
known-good year can be fine. But default posture is skeptical.

**Avoid by default**

- Jeep Compass / Patriot / Liberty (CVT failures, electrical issues)
- Dodge Journey / Dart / Avenger / Caliber / Neon
- Ford Focus and Fiesta with DCT (PowerShift dual-clutch failures, class
  action settled)
- Ford Fusion (Hybrid battery issues; 1.6L EcoBoost coolant intrusion)
- Chevrolet Cruze (1.4L turbo head gaskets, electrical)
- Chevrolet Cobalt / Aveo / Sonic / Spark
- Chevrolet Malibu (eAssist generation electrical; 2.5L oil consumption)
- Hyundai Sonata / Elantra / Veloster (Theta II 2.0/2.4 engine recalls
  for rod-bearing failure; verify recall completion before buying)
- Kia Optima / Forte / Soul / Sorento (same Theta II engine issues)
- Nissan with CVT: Altima, Sentra, Versa, Maxima, Rogue, Murano,
  Pathfinder (CVT failure is endemic, often before 100K)
- Mitsubishi anything (parts availability)
- Volkswagen / Audi non-TDI gas (timing chain tensioner failures on
  2.0 TSI; carbon buildup; expensive everything)
- BMW / Mercedes / Audi older than 8 years (maintenance cost trap unless
  user is wrenching)
- Mini Cooper (timing chain tensioner; expensive)
- Fiat 500
- Saturn / Pontiac (parts availability and dealer support gone)
- Smart Car

**Specific year/model traps**

- 2007 to 2009 Toyota 2AZ-FE engine (Camry, RAV4, Scion tC): oil-consumption
  TSB. Verify oil-use history.
- 2008 to 2012 Honda 4-cyl K24Z (Accord, CR-V): same oil-burn pattern.
- Pre-2012 Subaru EJ25: head gasket failure. Plan on replacement around
  150K if not already done.
- 2010 to 2015 Hyundai/Kia Theta II 2.0/2.4: rod-bearing recall. Confirm
  the recall has been completed before buying.
- Pre-2008 Honda V6 with 5-speed automatic (Odyssey, Pilot, Ridgeline,
  Accord V6): transmission failure pattern.
- 2009 to 2012 Ford 3.5L EcoBoost: water pump is internal and a $2K job.

## SUV scope notes

If the user wants an SUV, the reliable list is short. Domestic mid-size
and full-size SUVs are mostly Tier 3 or worse at this price point. Push
toward Honda CR-V, Toyota RAV4, Subaru Forester, Mazda CX-5, Lexus RX, or
Acura RDX.

## How to use this file

1. When the user pastes a listing, match the make/model against this list.
2. If Tier 1: continue to seller verification and known-issue lookup.
3. If Tier 2 (grandpa): same, with a note that fuel economy and ergonomics
   are the trade-off.
4. If Risky: tell the user this model has known issues. Name the specific
   issue. Recommend looking at Tier 1 alternatives at the same price.
5. Tier 3 absent from this list: look it up. If it is a European luxury car,
   a domestic compact, or a non-Toyota/Honda/Mazda hybrid, default to
   skeptical.
