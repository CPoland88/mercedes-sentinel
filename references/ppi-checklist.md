# Pre-purchase inspection (PPI) checklist

A PPI is an independent mechanic's inspection of a used car before the
buyer commits. Costs $100 to $200. Catches problems a test drive does
not, and gives the buyer a written list of defects to negotiate against.

**Always get a PPI.** Even on a Tier 1 car from a green-flag seller. The
PPI either confirms the buy decision or saves the user thousands. Skip
only if the user is shopping cars under $2,000, where the PPI cost is a
meaningful fraction of the purchase.

## Who does the PPI

In order of preference:

1. **Independent shop that specializes in the brand.** A Honda specialist
   knows the model's failure modes. Best for high-mile Hondas, Toyotas,
   Lexus. Hybrid specialists exist for Prius / Insight / Camry Hybrid.
2. **General independent shop with a good reputation.** Fine for most
   cars.
3. **Dealer service department for the brand.** Will sell the user every
   service possible, but the inspection itself is thorough. Twice the
   cost.
4. **Mobile mechanic.** Last resort. Convenient but the toolkit is
   limited; no lift means no underbody inspection.

Do **not** use a shop the seller recommends.

## Buyer's pre-PPI walk-around (do this before paying for the PPI)

Before paying for a professional inspection, do a 15-minute walk-around
yourself. This catches the obvious deal-breakers.

**Exterior:**
- Walk all four sides. Look for mismatched paint, uneven panel gaps,
  body filler waves under the sun.
- Tire tread depth (penny test or tread-wear bars). Tire age (DOT date
  code on sidewall, e.g., "1819" = week 18, 2019).
- Look for fresh undercoating or rust-converter spray (cover-up).
- Check all four corners are level (sagging = suspension or air-ride
  failure).

**Underbody (peek if you can):**
- Fluid leaks (oil, coolant, transmission, power steering).
- Rust on frame rails and subframe.
- Damage to skid plates or undercarriage from off-roading.

**Engine bay:**
- Oil cap: clean? Or sludge / "milkshake" emulsion (head gasket failure)?
- Coolant: clean and full? Or rust-colored / oily?
- Belts: cracked or glazed?
- Battery: corroded terminals?
- VIN on firewall and on dashboard match each other and match the title.

**Interior:**
- All electronics work: windows, locks, mirrors, sunroof, A/C cold, heat
  hot, all speakers, all warning lights cycle.
- Driver seat wear vs. claimed mileage (excessive wear on a "90K"
  car suggests odometer rollback).
- Carpet feels dry, no musty smell (flood damage).

**Test drive (always):**
- Cold start: any smoke from exhaust? Blue = oil burn, white = coolant
  burn, black = rich fuel mixture.
- Acceleration: any hesitation, lurching, or noise?
- Brakes: pull to one side? Pulsation in pedal (warped rotors)? Squeal?
- Steering: vibration at speed? Pulling? Clunks over bumps?
- Transmission: smooth shifts? Any flare or slip?
- Highway speed: tracks straight hands-off? Steering wheel centered?

If anything on this list fails, you don't even need the PPI yet. Decide
whether to walk or proceed.

## Professional PPI scope (by drivetrain)

The PPI should cover everything below. If the shop says they can do it
in less than 1 hour, find another shop.

### All cars

- Lift on a hoist; visual inspection of frame, subframe, suspension,
  exhaust, fuel and brake lines
- Brake pad and rotor measurement (pads in mm, rotors compared to
  minimum spec)
- Tire tread depth and DOT date (all four)
- Fluid quality (oil, coolant, transmission, brake, power steering)
- Engine OBD-II scan for stored AND pending codes (pending codes catch
  defects the seller cleared before the sale)
- Compression test if engine is high-mileage or runs roughly
  (industry threshold: 15% variance between cylinders, or any cylinder
  below 75% of spec)
- Camshaft and crankshaft seal inspection for leaks (Honda J-series
  and Toyota 2AZ are known leakers)
- Steering rack play check; ball joints, tie rods, control arm bushings
- AC system static pressure check (not just "blows cold"; pressures
  predict compressor failure)
- Test all electronics
- Road test under load (highway + back-roads)
- Verify VIN on engine block matches title (catches stolen-engine swaps)

### Internal combustion (gas)

- Spark plug condition (often gives clean read on combustion health)
- Vacuum leak check
- EVAP system check
- Check engine mounts and transmission mounts
- VVT actuator rattle on cold start (common Honda K-series tell)
- On direct-injection turbo engines: cam-to-crank correlation codes
  (P0008, P0016, P0017 etc.) which predict timing chain stretch

### Turbocharged

- Boost gauge reading at full throttle
- Oil quality (turbos are oil-cooled; bad oil = bad turbo)
- Intercooler piping leaks
- Carbon buildup on intake valves (problem for direct-injection engines
  like Audi 2.0T, Mazda SkyActiv, GM Ecotec)

### CVT-equipped (Nissan, Subaru, Honda Civic 2014+, Accord 2013+ 4-cyl, HR-V, Fit 2015+)

- CVT fluid color and smell (burned = walk)
- Test drive at multiple speeds; CVT should be smooth, no whining or
  shuddering
- Scan for CVT-specific codes
- If the car is Nissan with 80K+ miles and original CVT: factor in CVT
  replacement risk. Honda CVTs (Civic, Accord 4-cyl, HR-V) are
  meaningfully more reliable than Nissan Jatco units; still worth a
  fluid check but less catastrophic-failure-prone.

### Hybrid

- High-voltage battery state-of-health test via OBD-II (Foxwell NT510,
  Techstream for Toyota, HDS for Honda)
- Inverter coolant level, condition, and replacement history (Toyota
  spec is ~100K but real-world cars often have original at 150K+)
- HV transaxle fluid on Toyota hybrids (Prius, Camry Hybrid, Highlander
  Hybrid have their own gearbox fluid that is routinely ignored)
- 12V battery test (hybrids are sensitive; weak 12V causes phantom IMA
  faults). Prius 12V is an AGM in the cargo area, $250 to $350 OEM,
  not the standard $180 to $220 flooded.
- Verify hybrid-specific recalls completed (Prius Gen 3 brake actuator
  recall 16V-053, Insight IMA software)
- Listen for high-voltage cooling fan operation

### Diesel

- DPF status and regen history
- DEF system check (newer diesels)
- EGR cooler inspection
- Glow plug operation

### AWD

- Differential fluid condition (front, rear, transfer case)
- Tire wear should be even across all four (mismatched tires destroy
  AWD systems)

## Negotiable findings (use these)

Use the PPI report as a written defect list. For each finding, get a
cost estimate from the same shop. Knock the cost off the asking price.

- Brake pads under 4mm front or 5mm rear: $300 to $600 to replace pads
  and rotors
- Tires under 4/32": $400 to $800 for a set
- Coolant or oil leak: $200 to $1,500 depending on source
- Worn struts/shocks: $400 to $1,000
- CV axle boot torn: $200 per side
- Worn ball joints / tie rods / control arm bushings: $200 to $600 per
  item
- Cracked exhaust manifold or muffler: $300 to $900
- Battery near end of life: $180 to $250
- Upcoming timing belt (within 10K miles): $500 to $900 on a 4-cyl;
  $900 to $1,600 on a V6 with water pump bundle

## Walk-away findings (don't negotiate, just leave)

- Compression below 80% of spec on any cylinder
- Head gasket failure indicators (coolant in oil, oil in coolant,
  bubbles in radiator)
- Transmission slipping or flaring under load
- CVT shuddering, whining, or burned fluid
- Frame rust on structural members
- Hidden accident damage (welds, body filler, paint mismatch)
- Mismatched VIN (engine block VIN doesn't match title)
- Hybrid battery failing state-of-health test (only if user is not
  prepared to budget for the replacement)
- Active check engine light the seller "cleared" before the PPI and that
  returns within 50 miles

## After the PPI

The shop gives a written report. Bring it to the seller. Three outcomes:

1. **Buy as-is** because the report is clean. Move to negotiation +
   close.
2. **Buy at reduced price** with the defect cost knocked off. Use the
   shop's repair estimate, not Internet averages.
3. **Walk.** Politely. Thank the seller. There are always more listings.
